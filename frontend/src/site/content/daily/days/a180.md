## Understand

### The presentation

The capstone ends by telling people what you found. A presentation is not a tour of the work. It answers the question first, then gives the evidence, then says what to do. For ten minutes, six to eight slides are enough. A reliable structure is: the question and why it matters, the data and how you made it trustworthy, one slide per finding (a claim with its number, base and caveat), the recommendation and what would change it, and the limits with next steps. Each slide makes one point, and its title states that point as a sentence ("Email returns the most net revenue per unit of spend") rather than naming a topic ("Channels"). A slide with no number is a decoration unless it is the title or the closing slide.

### Time and words

A slide that is read aloud takes about as long as it has words, so a slide with fifty words costs forty seconds of silence or reading. Keep messages short (under about fifteen words) and put the detail in what you say and in the README. Plan minutes per slide and total them against the time you have, including a minute for questions: a ten-minute slot is a nine-minute talk. Rehearse once aloud with a timer, because the rehearsal reveals the slide that runs long.

### Feedback

Ask reviewers to score the same criteria so their answers can be compared: clarity of the question, strength of evidence, honesty about caveats and usefulness of the recommendation. Average the scores per criterion and find the lowest, which is where the next hour of work should go. Then read the comments for the reason behind a low score: a low evidence score might mean a missing base, not a missing analysis. Treat feedback as data: record it, summarise it, act on the biggest gap, and do not argue with it during the session.

### Retrospective

A retrospective asks three questions about the project: what worked and should be repeated, what did not work and why, and what you would change next time. Good answers are specific and tied to evidence. "Cleaning with a row log made the reconciliation easy" is useful. "It went well" is not. For this capstone, typical entries are that scoping first saved rework, that a join at the wrong grain would have multiplied spend, and that generating narrative numbers from code prevented drift. The retrospective belongs in the README or a short note, because it shows how you think about your own work.

### Reviewing the programme

The programme taught an analyst's skills in layers: Python and pandas for working with data, statistics for judging uncertainty, SQL for querying a database, Excel, Power Query, DAX and Power BI for business reporting, visualisation and storytelling for communication, data quality and ETL basics for trust, Git and testing for reproducibility, and interview practice for presenting yourself. A skill review lists each area with an honest rating from one to five. The areas rated lowest are not failures: they decide where the next study hours go. A rating is only useful with evidence, such as "I wrote the window query without help" or "I needed the notes for every merge".

|---|---|
| `sum(iterable)` | Total of the values |

| `round(x, 1)` | Number rounded to one decimal |

## Learn by example

### Example 1: Build and check the presentation

The slides are built from the verified analysis numbers. The check totals the minutes against a nine-minute talk, tests each message for length and requires a number on every content slide.

```python run
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd


def generate(seed=7):
    """Seeded online-store data with planted defects: 500 orders, 120 customers, 20 products."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    customers = pd.DataFrame({
        "customer_id": np.arange(1, 121),
        "country": rng.choice(["DE", "FR", "UK", "US"], 120, p=[0.3, 0.2, 0.2, 0.3]),
        "signup_date": pd.Timestamp("2024-07-01") + pd.to_timedelta(rng.integers(0, 180, 120), unit="D"),
    })
    customers.loc[rng.choice(120, 8, replace=False), "country"] = "us "
    products = pd.DataFrame({
        "product_id": np.arange(1, 21),
        "category": np.repeat(["home", "beauty", "sports", "electronics"], 5),
        "unit_price": (10 + rng.random(20) * 90).round(2),
    })
    campaigns = pd.DataFrame({
        "campaign_id": [0, 1, 2, 3],
        "channel": ["organic", "email", "paid_search", "social"],
        "spend": [0.0, 800.0, 2500.0, 1200.0],
    })
    n = 500
    orders = pd.DataFrame({
        "order_id": np.arange(1001, 1001 + n),
        "customer_id": rng.integers(1, 121, n),
        "product_id": rng.integers(1, 21, n),
        "order_date": pd.Timestamp("2025-01-01") + pd.to_timedelta(rng.integers(0, 180, n), unit="D"),
        "quantity": rng.integers(1, 5, n),
        "discount_pct": rng.choice([0, 10, 20], n, p=[0.6, 0.25, 0.15]),
        "campaign_id": rng.choice([0, 1, 2, 3], n, p=[0.4, 0.2, 0.25, 0.15]),
    })
    orders.loc[rng.choice(n, 5, replace=False), "quantity"] = -1
    orders.loc[rng.choice(n, 6, replace=False), "customer_id"] = 999
    orders = pd.concat([orders, orders.sample(10, random_state=1)], ignore_index=True)
    returned = rng.choice(np.arange(1001, 1001 + n), 60, replace=False)
    returns = pd.DataFrame({
        "order_id": np.concatenate([returned, [9001, 9002, 9003]]),
        "reason": rng.choice(["damaged", "wrong_item", "changed_mind"], 63, p=[0.3, 0.2, 0.5]),
    })
    return {"orders": orders, "customers": customers, "products": products, "campaigns": campaigns, "returns": returns}



def clean(t):
    """Apply the quality rules; return cleaned tables and a row-accounting log."""
    log = {}
    o = t["orders"]
    log["raw_orders"] = len(o)
    o = o.drop_duplicates()
    log["after_duplicates"] = len(o)
    o = o[o["quantity"] > 0]
    log["after_quantity"] = len(o)
    c = t["customers"].copy()
    c["country"] = c["country"].str.strip().str.upper()
    o = o[o["customer_id"].isin(c["customer_id"])]
    log["after_customers"] = len(o)
    r = t["returns"]
    log["raw_returns"] = len(r)
    r = r[r["order_id"].isin(o["order_id"])].drop_duplicates("order_id")
    log["clean_returns"] = len(r)
    p = t["products"]
    o = o.merge(p[["product_id", "unit_price"]], on="product_id", how="left")
    o["revenue"] = (o["quantity"] * o["unit_price"] * (1 - o["discount_pct"] / 100)).round(2)
    o["returned"] = o["order_id"].isin(r["order_id"])
    return {"orders": o.reset_index(drop=True), "customers": c, "products": p, "campaigns": t["campaigns"], "returns": r.reset_index(drop=True)}, log



def prepare(ct):
    """Analysis tables from the clean data: orders with category, channel and month, plus the three chart series."""
    o = ct["orders"].merge(ct["products"][["product_id", "category"]], on="product_id")
    o = o.merge(ct["campaigns"][["campaign_id", "channel"]], on="campaign_id")
    o["month"] = o["order_date"].dt.month
    net = o[~o["returned"]]
    net_month = net.groupby("month")["revenue"].sum().round(2)
    spend = ct["campaigns"].set_index("channel")["spend"]
    roas = (net.groupby("channel")["revenue"].sum() / spend[spend > 0]).dropna().round(2)
    rate = (o.groupby("category")["returned"].mean() * 100).round(1)
    return o, net_month, roas, rate


ct, log = clean(generate())
o, net_month, roas, rate = prepare(ct)
peak = net_month.idxmax()
slides = [
    {"title": "Which channels and categories should the store act on?", "number": None, "minutes": 1},
    {"title": f"{len(o)} of {log['raw_orders']} raw order rows survive five rule checks", "number": len(o), "minutes": 1},
    {"title": f"May is the strongest month at {net_month[peak]:.2f} net revenue", "number": net_month[peak], "minutes": 2},
    {"title": f"Email returns {roas.max():.2f} net revenue per unit of spend", "number": roas.max(), "minutes": 2},
    {"title": f"{rate.idxmax().capitalize()} has the highest return rate at {rate.max():.1f}%", "number": rate.max(), "minutes": 1},
    {"title": "Test moving budget to email for one month", "number": None, "minutes": 2},
]
total = sum(s["minutes"] for s in slides)
print("slides:", len(slides), "| minutes:", total, "| fits nine-minute talk:", total <= 9)
for i, s in enumerate(slides, 1):
    words = len(s["title"].split())
    needs_number = 1 < i < len(slides)
    ok = words <= 15 and (s["number"] is not None or not needs_number)
    print(f"{i}. {words} words, {s['minutes']} min, {'ok' if ok else 'fix'}")
```

```output
1. 9 words, 1 min, ok
2. 10 words, 1 min, ok
3. 9 words, 2 min, ok
4. 9 words, 2 min, ok
5. 8 words, 1 min, ok
6. 8 words, 2 min, ok
```

### Example 2: Summarise reviewer feedback

Three reviewers score four criteria from 1 to 5. The summary gives the mean per criterion, finds the lowest and lists actions from the criteria below 4.

```python run
scores = {
    "clarity of question": [5, 4, 5],
    "strength of evidence": [4, 4, 3],
    "honesty about caveats": [3, 4, 3],
    "usefulness of recommendation": [4, 3, 4],
}
means = {k: round(sum(v) / len(v), 2) for k, v in scores.items()}
for k, v in means.items():
    print(f"{k}: {v}")
lowest = min(means, key=means.get)
print("lowest:", lowest)
print("actions:", [k for k, v in sorted(means.items(), key=lambda kv: (kv[1], kv[0])) if v < 4])
```

```output
clarity of question: 4.67
strength of evidence: 3.67
honesty about caveats: 3.33
usefulness of recommendation: 3.67
lowest: honesty about caveats
actions: ['honesty about caveats', 'strength of evidence', 'usefulness of recommendation']
```

### Example 3: Skill review and learning plan

Each area of the programme is rated from 1 to 5. The gap to 5 is computed, areas rated below 3.5 are flagged, and the plan lists the biggest gaps first with a share of a twenty-hour study budget.

```python run
ratings = {
    "Python and pandas": 4, "Statistics": 3, "SQL": 4, "Excel and Power Query": 3,
    "DAX and Power BI": 2, "Visualisation and storytelling": 4, "Data quality and ETL": 4,
    "Git and testing": 3, "Interview practice": 3,
}
gaps = {k: 5 - v for k, v in ratings.items()}
plan = sorted(gaps.items(), key=lambda kv: (-kv[1], kv[0]))
total_gap = sum(gaps.values())
print("average rating:", round(sum(ratings.values()) / len(ratings), 2))
print("flagged below 3.5:", [k for k, v in ratings.items() if v < 3.5])
for k, gap in plan[:4]:
    print(f"{k}: gap {gap}, {20 * gap / total_gap:.1f} hours")
```

```output
average rating: 3.33
flagged below 3.5: ['Statistics', 'Excel and Power Query', 'DAX and Power BI', 'Git and testing', 'Interview practice']
DAX and Power BI: gap 3, 4.0 hours
Excel and Power Query: gap 2, 2.7 hours
Git and testing: gap 2, 2.7 hours
Interview practice: gap 2, 2.7 hours
```

## Real-world applications

- An analyst presents a quarterly analysis in ten minutes with an answer-first structure and a number on every content slide, as in Example 1, so decision makers act on it.
- A team collects scored feedback after a demo and fixes the lowest criterion first, the method of Example 2, instead of reacting to the loudest comment.

## Daily review

### Key takeaways

- Present the answer first; one point per slide; titles are sentences; content slides carry a number.
- Budget minutes before you build, and rehearse once with a timer.
- Score feedback on shared criteria and fix the lowest first.
- Rate skills with evidence, and turn the gaps into a plan with a deliverable and a date.

### Common mistakes

- Narrating the process instead of the findings: the audience waits for the point. Start with the answer.
- Slides full of text that you read out: the audience reads faster than you speak. Keep messages short.
- Skipping the timed rehearsal: the talk overruns and the recommendation is cut. Rehearse and trim.
- Rating every skill 4 without evidence: the plan is then empty. Use evidence such as "needed the notes" to justify a rating.
