## Objectives

- Write star-join queries that join the fact table to several dimensions and group by their attributes, with the totals checked against the fact table.
- Use window functions on grouped results: share of total within a month, rank within a group, previous-period change with `LAG`, running totals.
- Report history correctly on a type 2 dimension: sales by the country at the time of the sale versus by the customer's current country.
- Package business definitions as a reporting view so that every dashboard computes revenue the same way.
- Recognise and avoid the common reporting errors: fan-out joins, mixed grains, and dividing by zero in ratios.

## Understand

### The payoff of the model

Days 144 to 150 built the warehouse: facts at a declared grain, conformed dimensions with surrogate keys, history in the customer dimension, partitions and aggregates. This lesson uses it. The pattern of an analytical query on a star is so regular that it has a name, the **star join**: put the fact table at the centre, join each dimension needed on its surrogate key, group by the descriptive attributes, aggregate the additive measures. The regularity is the point of the design. A question about revenue by country and category and month needs three joins and no thought about how the tables relate, and the same pattern answers the next question after changing only the `SELECT` and `GROUP BY` lists.

### Reading a star-join query

Read it in four layers. The **grain** of the result is set by the `GROUP BY`: one row per (month, category, country). The **measures** are aggregates of fact columns (`sum(f.amount_cents)`, `count(*)` for order lines, `sum(f.quantity)` for units). The **dimension attributes** in `SELECT` and `GROUP BY` come from the joined dimensions. And the **filters** in `WHERE` restrict facts (a date range on `d.full_date`, `p.category = 'Books'`); because the dimensions are joined by their keys, filters on dimension attributes are as cheap to write as filters on the fact. Always check a result against the fact table: the sum of the result's measure over all groups must equal `sum(amount_cents)` of the fact table for the same filters, or a join has dropped or multiplied rows (Day 144).

### Windows on top of aggregates

A report seldom wants raw group totals alone. It wants each group in context, which is what **window functions** (Day 112) provide on top of a grouped query:

- the **share** of a group within its parent: `100.0 * cents / sum(cents) OVER (PARTITION BY month)`,
- the **rank** within the parent: `rank() OVER (PARTITION BY month ORDER BY cents DESC)`, and filtering `rank = 1` gives the top group per month,
- the **change** from the previous period: `cents - lag(cents) OVER (PARTITION BY category ORDER BY month)`, or the percentage change, guarded against a zero previous value with `NULLIF(previous, 0)` so that the division yields NULL instead of an error,
- a **running total** within a year: `sum(cents) OVER (PARTITION BY year ORDER BY month)`.

Window functions run after `GROUP BY`, so they can use its aggregates, and `PARTITION BY` resets the calculation for each parent group. When a series has holes (a category with no sales in a month), `LAG` compares with the previous existing row, not the previous calendar month: build the full grid first (a cross join of months and categories) when that matters.

### History in reports

On a type 2 customer dimension a report can answer two different questions, and the join decides which. Joining the fact to the dimension on the surrogate key, `f.customer_key = c.customer_key`, gives **the country at the time of the sale** (as-was), because each fact points at the version that was valid then. To report by the customer's **current** country (as-is), look up the current row of the same business key: join `dim_customer c` on `f.customer_key`, then join `dim_customer cur` on `cur.customer_id = c.customer_id AND cur.is_current`. Neither is the wrong answer; they are different questions, and a report should say which it uses. Customer counts must use `count(DISTINCT customer_id)`, never `count(DISTINCT customer_key)`, because one customer can have several keys.

### Reporting views

A **view** (Day 112) with the business definition makes the answer consistent. `rpt_monthly_category` could define revenue as the sum of `amount_cents` of the fact, add the month label from the date dimension and the category from the product dimension, and expose columns with business names. Dashboards then select from the view and never repeat the joins, and when the definition changes (say, returns are subtracted) it changes in one place. A view stores no data; for expensive definitions put a materialised view or aggregate table (Day 150) behind it. Name the view for the grain and the audience (`rpt_` prefix), document it, and treat it as a contract.

### Common errors in analytical SQL

**Fan-out**: joining a fact to a table with several rows per key multiplies the facts. **Mixed grains**: summing a measure stored at order level over order-line rows repeats it. **Division by zero**: a ratio over an empty group raises an error unless guarded with `NULLIF`. **Percentages of percentages**: share of a share is not a share; compute ratios from sums. **Unordered output**: without `ORDER BY` the row order is undefined, so reports and tests must sort. **Counting rows as customers**: `count(*)` counts order lines.

### Common misconception

Learners believe that the more tables a query joins, the more likely it is wrong. Joins on surrogate keys to dimensions with one row per key are safe: they neither drop nor multiply facts. The risk comes from joins to tables that are not dimensions of that fact, and from inner joins with missing keys. Verify with a total.

### Why it matters

The warehouse exists to answer questions like these quickly and consistently. A report with a wrong denominator or a doubled total is worse than no report, because people act on it.

## Syntax and key functions

```sql-syntax
SELECT d.month, p.category,
       sum(f.amount_cents) AS cents,
       round(100.0 * sum(f.amount_cents) / sum(sum(f.amount_cents)) OVER (PARTITION BY d.month), 1) AS share_pct,
       rank() OVER (PARTITION BY d.month ORDER BY sum(f.amount_cents) DESC) AS month_rank,
       sum(f.amount_cents) - lag(sum(f.amount_cents)) OVER (PARTITION BY p.category ORDER BY d.month) AS change
FROM fact_sales f
JOIN dim_date d ON d.date_key = f.date_key
JOIN dim_product p ON p.product_key = f.product_key
GROUP BY d.month, p.category;
```

```sql-syntax
CREATE VIEW rpt_monthly_category AS
SELECT d.year, d.month, p.category, count(*) AS lines, sum(f.quantity) AS units, sum(f.amount_cents) AS cents
FROM fact_sales f JOIN dim_date d ON d.date_key = f.date_key JOIN dim_product p ON p.product_key = f.product_key
GROUP BY d.year, d.month, p.category;
```

| Item | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Star join | Fact joined to dimensions on surrogate keys, grouped by attributes. |
| `sum(x) OVER (PARTITION BY g)` | Total per parent group, for shares. |
| `rank() OVER (PARTITION BY g ORDER BY x DESC)` | Position within the group. |
| `lag(x) OVER (PARTITION BY g ORDER BY t)` | Previous period's value. |
| `NULLIF(x, 0)` | NULL instead of zero, to avoid division errors. |
| As-was vs as-is | Join on the fact's `customer_key` vs on the current row of the business key. |
| `count(DISTINCT customer_id)` | Customers, not versions. |
| `CREATE VIEW rpt_...` | Reporting view with the business definition. |

## Learn by example

### Example 1: A three-dimension star join, checked against the fact

Revenue by month, category and customer country for January. The query returns the first rows and the second query verifies that the groups add up to the fact total for January: a check that no join dropped or multiplied rows.

```sql run
CREATE TABLE dim_date (date_key integer PRIMARY KEY, full_date date NOT NULL, year integer NOT NULL, month integer NOT NULL);
CREATE TABLE dim_customer (customer_key integer PRIMARY KEY, customer_id text NOT NULL, customer_name text NOT NULL, country text NOT NULL,
    valid_from date NOT NULL, valid_to date, is_current boolean NOT NULL);
CREATE TABLE dim_product (product_key integer PRIMARY KEY, product_id text NOT NULL, product_name text NOT NULL, category text NOT NULL);
CREATE TABLE fact_sales (sale_id integer PRIMARY KEY, date_key integer NOT NULL REFERENCES dim_date, customer_key integer NOT NULL REFERENCES dim_customer,
    product_key integer NOT NULL REFERENCES dim_product, quantity integer NOT NULL, amount_cents integer NOT NULL);
INSERT INTO dim_date SELECT to_char(d, 'YYYYMMDD')::int, d::date, extract(year FROM d)::int, extract(month FROM d)::int
FROM generate_series(date '2026-01-01', date '2026-02-28', interval '1 day') AS d;
INSERT INTO dim_customer SELECT i, 'C' || lpad(i::text, 3, '0'), 'Customer ' || lpad(i::text, 2, '0'),
    (ARRAY['PT', 'ES', 'FR', 'DE'])[i % 4 + 1], date '2026-01-01', NULL, true FROM generate_series(1, 12) AS i;
INSERT INTO dim_product SELECT i, 'P' || lpad(i::text, 3, '0'), 'Product ' || i,
    (ARRAY['Stationery', 'Books', 'Tools', 'Toys'])[i % 4 + 1] FROM generate_series(1, 8) AS i;
INSERT INTO fact_sales SELECT i, to_char(date '2026-01-01' + i * 3 % 59, 'YYYYMMDD')::int, i * 5 % 12 + 1, i * 3 % 8 + 1, i % 4 + 1,
    (i % 4 + 1) * (500 + (i * 3 % 8 + 1) * 250) FROM generate_series(1, 60) AS i;
SELECT d.month, p.category, c.country, count(*) AS lines, sum(f.amount_cents) AS cents

JOIN dim_date d ON d.date_key = f.date_key
```

### Example 2: Share, rank and top group per month

For each month and category the query computes revenue, its share of the month and its rank in the month. Filtering on `rank = 1` in an outer query gives the leading category of each month. Percentages are computed from sums, not from other percentages.

### Example 3: Change from the previous month

Month-over-month change per category with `lag`, and the percentage change guarded against a zero previous value with `NULLIF`. January has no previous month, so its change is NULL. The result is limited to the rows of February, which are the interesting ones.

```sql run

CREATE TABLE dim_customer (customer_key integer PRIMARY KEY, customer_id text NOT NULL, customer_name text NOT NULL, country text NOT NULL,

    (ARRAY['PT', 'ES', 'FR', 'DE'])[i % 4 + 1], date '2026-01-01', NULL, true FROM generate_series(1, 12) AS i;
INSERT INTO dim_product SELECT i, 'P' || lpad(i::text, 3, '0'), 'Product ' || i,
    (ARRAY['Stationery', 'Books', 'Tools', 'Toys'])[i % 4 + 1] FROM generate_series(1, 8) AS i;
INSERT INTO fact_sales SELECT i, to_char(date '2026-01-01' + i * 3 % 59, 'YYYYMMDD')::int, i * 5 % 12 + 1, i * 3 % 8 + 1, i % 4 + 1,
    (i % 4 + 1) * (500 + (i * 3 % 8 + 1) * 250) FROM generate_series(1, 60) AS i;
WITH m AS (
    SELECT d.month, p.category, sum(f.amount_cents) AS cents
    FROM fact_sales f JOIN dim_date d ON d.date_key = f.date_key JOIN dim_product p ON p.product_key = f.product_key
    GROUP BY d.month, p.category
), c AS (
    SELECT month, category, cents, lag(cents) OVER (PARTITION BY category ORDER BY month) AS previous FROM m
)
SELECT category, previous, cents, cents - previous AS change,
       round(100.0 * (cents - previous) / NULLIF(previous, 0), 1) AS change_pct
FROM c WHERE month = 2 ORDER BY category;
```

### Example 4: As-was and as-is countries on a type 2 dimension

Customer `C003` moved from Germany to Spain on 1 February: key 3 is closed and key 13 inserted, and two February sales use key 13. The first column of each row is the country at the time of the sale (join on the fact's key), the second the customer's current country (join to the current row of the business key). Sales made before the move show `DE` as-was and `ES` as-is.


### Example 5: A reporting view and a query on it

The view `rpt_monthly_category` holds the business definition. A dashboard query selects the best category per month from it with a window, without repeating any join. Queries on the view are ordinary queries, and the view can later be replaced by a materialised view without changing them.

```sql run

INSERT INTO dim_product SELECT i, 'P' || lpad(i::text, 3, '0'), 'Product ' || i,
    (ARRAY['Stationery', 'Books', 'Tools', 'Toys'])[i % 4 + 1] FROM generate_series(1, 8) AS i;
```

## Real-world applications

- **Monthly business review.** Revenue by category and country with share of month and change from last month is built from one star join and two windows.
- **Regional targets.** The as-was country attributes each sale to the region that applied then, so target attainment is not distorted when customers move.
- **Self-service reporting.** A reporting view per subject area gives analysts the joins and the definitions already written.
- **Reconciliation.** The check of group sums against the fact total is a standing test on every report query.

## Daily review

### Key takeaways

- A star-join query: fact in the centre, dimensions on surrogate keys, group by attributes, aggregate additive measures.
- Always check group totals against the fact total.
- Windows on grouped results give share, rank, change from the previous period and running totals.
- Join on the fact's key for as-was, to the current row of the business key for as-is; count customers by business key.
- Put business definitions in reporting views; guard ratios with `NULLIF`.

### Common mistakes

- **Joining a non-dimension table.** The fact rows multiply. Verify with a total.
- **Dividing by a possibly zero value.** The query errors. Use `NULLIF(denominator, 0)`.
- **`count(DISTINCT customer_key)` for customers.** Versions inflate the count. Use `customer_id`.
- **Share of a share.** Compute ratios from sums.
- **No `ORDER BY`.** Row order is undefined; sort every report query.
