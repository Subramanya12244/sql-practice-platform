## Understand

### What a PivotTable does

A **PivotTable** is an interactive summary of a table. You choose which column becomes the row labels, which becomes the column labels and which is added up, and Excel produces the grid. It is the point-and-click version of what you wrote on Day 95 with one `SUMIFS` per cell, and of the `pivot_table` and `groupby` calls from the pandas days. The important differences are that you build it by dragging, you can change its shape in seconds, and it does not recalculate by itself when the data changes.

A PivotTable works on a **source**: ideally an Excel Table (Day 91) because it grows when rows are added. The source needs a clean header row and no blank columns, as the hygiene rules require; a column with no header name stops a pivot from being created. Excel copies the source into a hidden **pivot cache** when the pivot is made. The pivot reads the cache, not the sheet, which is the reason it shows old numbers until you refresh it.

### Building one

Click inside the Table and choose Insert, then PivotTable. Excel proposes the Table as the source and a new worksheet as the place. The PivotTable Fields pane lists the column names, with four areas below it. **Rows** puts each distinct value of a field on its own row. **Columns** gives each distinct value its own column. **Values** holds the numbers to summarise. **Filters** adds a drop-down above the pivot that restricts the whole report. Dragging `Region` into Rows and `Amount` into Values gives the total amount per region. Adding `Product` to Columns gives a two-way grid, with totals on the right and at the bottom called **grand totals**. These grand totals can be switched off on the Design tab.

### Summarising values

A field in Values has a **summary function**. Sum adds, Count counts the non-empty cells, Count Numbers counts numeric cells only, and Average, Max, Min and Product are also available. Right-click a value, choose Value Field Settings and pick one. Excel chooses Sum when every cell of the field is numeric, and chooses **Count** when the column contains text or blanks. A column of amounts where one number was typed as text therefore appears as "Count of Amount", with a count of rows instead of a total. This is the pivot's version of the text-number problem from Day 91, and the fix is to repair the data and refresh.

The second tab of Value Field Settings, Show Values As, expresses a value relative to something else: % of Grand Total, % of Column Total, % of Row Total, or the difference from a previous item. The stored value does not change, only its presentation. You can also put the same field in Values twice, once as a sum and once as a percentage.

### Grouping

Select a date in the Rows area, right-click and choose Group. You can group by Months, Quarters and Years, and combine them; Excel adds helper fields with the names `Months`, `Quarters` and `Years`. Choose Years together with Months, otherwise January of two different years is added into one row. Recent versions of Excel may group dates automatically when you add a date field. A date field with a text date, or with blanks, cannot be grouped, which is another consequence of the hygiene rules. Numbers can be grouped as well: right-click, Group, and give a start, an end and a step, for example by 100, to make ranges 0 to 99, 100 to 199 and so on. Items of a text field can be grouped by selecting several and choosing Group Selection, which is a manual grouping.

### Calculated fields

A **calculated field** is a new field defined by a formula on other fields, such as `=Amount/Qty`. Create it from PivotTable Analyze, Fields, Items & Sets, Calculated Field. The key point is that it is evaluated on the **summed** values of the fields, not row by row. In a row of the pivot where Amount sums to 200 and Qty sums to 10, the calculated field is 200/10 = 20. That is the weighted average price, and it is not the same as the average of the prices of the individual rows. Learners often assume the opposite, and the numbers disagree whenever the quantities differ. A calculated field cannot refer to cells or use lookup functions; it can use the field names and arithmetic.

### Slicers and filters

A **slicer** is a set of buttons that filter a PivotTable. Choose PivotTable Analyze, Insert Slicer, and tick a field such as Region. Clicking a button filters the pivot to that item, and holding `Ctrl` selects several. A slicer can be connected to more than one PivotTable (Report Connections), so one click filters a whole dashboard, the idea that the next lesson uses. A slicer does the same job as a Filters drop-down, with a visible state. A **timeline** is the date version.

### Refreshing

A pivot does not watch its source. After the data changes, right-click the pivot and choose Refresh, or choose Data, then Refresh All (`Ctrl+Alt+F5`) to refresh every pivot and every connection. If the source is an Excel Table, new rows are picked up by the refresh. If the source is a plain range, new rows under the range are not included until you change the data source. A refresh can also reset column widths unless you untick "Autofit column widths on update". Pivot cells are output, not input: type a number over one and Excel refuses, and write formulas that point at the pivot with `GETPIVOTDATA`, which Excel inserts when you click a pivot cell from a formula.

### Why it matters

A PivotTable is usually the fastest way to answer "what happened, by what" in a workbook, and checking a pivot against `SUMIFS` or `groupby` is the standard way to prove that a summary is complete. The common misconception is that the pivot is always up to date; it is only as current as the last refresh.

## Syntax and key functions

There is no formula syntax for building a PivotTable; it is built from the Fields pane. The reference is the four areas and the settings you will use most.

| Area or setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| Rows | One row per distinct value of the field. |
| Columns | One column per distinct value. |
| Values | The field to summarise, with Sum, Count, Average and so on. |
| Filters | A drop-down above the pivot that filters everything. |
| Value Field Settings | Choose the summary function and the Show Values As option. |
| Group | Group dates (months, quarters, years) or numbers (start, end, step). |
| Calculated Field | A formula on fields, evaluated on their totals. |
| Slicer / Timeline | Buttons that filter one or several pivots. |
| Refresh / Refresh All | Re-read the source into the pivot cache. |

To read a pivot cell in a formula, click the pivot cell while typing `=`:

```excel
=GETPIVOTDATA("Amount", $A$3, "Region", "North")
```

The arguments are the data field name, any cell of the pivot, and pairs of field and item. The pandas counterparts are:

```python
df.pivot_table(index="Region", columns="Product", values="Amount", aggfunc="sum", margins=True)
df.groupby("Region")["Amount"].agg(["sum", "count", "mean"])
```

## Learn by example

### Example 1: Rows, Columns and Values

Take the sales Table from Day 95 (Region, Product, Qty, Amount, plus a date).

| Date | Region | Product | Qty | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-01-05 | North | Pen | 3 | 30 |
| 2025-01-20 | South | Pen | 1 | 10 |
| 2025-02-03 | North | Book | 2 | 40 |
| 2025-02-14 | East | Book | 5 | 100 |
| 2025-03-02 | North | Pen | 4 | 40 |
| 2025-03-15 | South | Book | 1 | 20 |
| 2025-03-28 | East | Pen | 2 | 20 |

Put Region in Rows, Product in Columns and Amount (Sum) in Values.

```python run
import pandas as pd

sales = pd.DataFrame({
    "Date": pd.to_datetime(["2025-01-05", "2025-01-20", "2025-02-03", "2025-02-14", "2025-03-02", "2025-03-15", "2025-03-28"]),
    "Region": ["North", "South", "North", "East", "North", "South", "East"],
    "Product": ["Pen", "Pen", "Book", "Book", "Pen", "Book", "Pen"],
    "Qty": [3, 1, 2, 5, 4, 1, 2],
    "Amount": [30, 10, 40, 100, 40, 20, 20],
})
pt = sales.pivot_table(index="Region", columns="Product", values="Amount", aggfunc="sum", margins=True, margins_name="Grand Total", fill_value=0)
print(pt.to_string())
```

```output
Product      Book  Pen  Grand Total
Region                             
East          100   20          120
North          40   70          110
South          20   10           30
Grand Total   160  100          260
```

North sells 40 in books and 70 in pens, for a row total of 110, and the grand total is 260. The pandas `margins=True` is the Grand Totals option. Differences: a PivotTable shows an empty cell for a combination with no rows (unless you set "For empty cells show"), while pandas gives `NaN` unless `fill_value` is set; and the pivot sorts the row items alphabetically by default, as `pivot_table` does.

### Example 2: Value Field Settings

Each setting is one more `aggfunc`. For the same table: Sum, Count, Average of Amount per region, and the percentage of the grand total.

```python run
import pandas as pd

sales = pd.DataFrame({
    "Region": ["North", "South", "North", "East", "North", "South", "East"],
    "Amount": [30, 10, 40, 100, 40, 20, 20],
})
g = sales.groupby("Region")["Amount"].agg(Sum="sum", Count="count", Average="mean")
g["PctOfTotal"] = (g["Sum"] / g["Sum"].sum() * 100).round(1)
print(g.round(2).to_string())

# one amount typed as text: what the pivot would count
amounts = pd.Series([30, 10, "40", 100])
numeric = amounts[amounts.map(lambda v: isinstance(v, (int, float)))]
print(len(amounts), len(numeric), numeric.sum())
```

```output
        Sum  Count  Average  PctOfTotal
Region                                 
East    120      2    60.00        46.2
North   110      3    36.67        42.3
South    30      2    15.00        11.5
4 3 140
```

East totals 120 (46.2% of 260), North 110 and South 30. In the last block, one of four values is text. Excel sees a text cell in the field and defaults to "Count of Amount", which would show 4 for the whole column; with Sum selected by hand it adds only the three numbers (140). Pandas needs `pd.to_numeric` to repair such a column.

### Example 3: Grouping dates and numbers

Group Date by Months and Quarters, and group Amount by 50.

```python run
import pandas as pd

sales = pd.DataFrame({
    "Date": pd.to_datetime(["2025-01-05", "2025-01-20", "2025-02-03", "2025-02-14", "2025-03-02", "2025-03-15", "2025-03-28"]),
    "Amount": [30, 10, 40, 100, 40, 20, 20],
})
by_month = sales.groupby(sales["Date"].dt.to_period("M"))["Amount"].sum()
print(by_month.to_string())
print(sales["Date"].dt.quarter.value_counts().to_dict())

bins = pd.cut(sales["Amount"], bins=[0, 50, 100, 150], right=False)
print(sales.groupby(bins, observed=False)["Amount"].agg(["count", "sum"]).to_string())
```

```output
Date
2025-01     40
2025-02    140
2025-03     80
Freq: M
{1: 7}
            count  sum
Amount                
[0, 50)         6  160
[50, 100)       0    0
[100, 150)      1  100
```

January totals 40, February 140 and March 80. All seven orders are in quarter 1. The ranges start at 0 and step by 50: [0, 50) holds six amounts (30, 10, 40, 40, 20, 20) totalling 160, and [50, 100) holds none. The amount 100 falls in [100, 150). Excel's number grouping uses the same lower-bound-included idea. In a pivot, grouping by Months alone would merge January 2025 and January 2026; `to_period("M")` keeps the year, which is why you also select Years in Excel.

### Example 4: A calculated field is a ratio of sums

Define `AvgPrice = Amount/Qty`. In a pivot with Region in Rows, the value is computed from each region's summed Amount and summed Qty.

```python run
import pandas as pd

sales = pd.DataFrame({
    "Region": ["North", "South", "North", "East", "North", "South", "East"],
    "Qty": [3, 1, 2, 5, 4, 1, 2],
    "Amount": [30, 10, 40, 100, 40, 20, 20],
})
g = sales.groupby("Region")[["Amount", "Qty"]].sum()
g["CalculatedField"] = (g["Amount"] / g["Qty"]).round(2)
sales["Price"] = sales["Amount"] / sales["Qty"]
g["MeanOfRowPrices"] = sales.groupby("Region")["Price"].mean().round(2)
print(g.to_string())
```

```output
        Amount  Qty  CalculatedField  MeanOfRowPrices
Region                                               
East       120    7            17.14            15.00
North      110    9            12.22            13.33
South       30    2            15.00            15.00
```

For North, 110 / 9 gives 12.22, but the mean of the three row prices (10, 20 and 10) is 13.33. A calculated field gives the first, the weighted average. If you need the mean of row prices you must add a `Price` column to the Table and average that column in the pivot.

### Example 5: Slicer and refresh

A slicer set to `North` shows the North rows only. A new order is then added to the Table. Until Refresh is clicked, the pivot is unchanged.

```python run
import pandas as pd

sales = pd.DataFrame({"Region": ["North", "South", "North"], "Amount": [30, 10, 40]})
cache = sales.copy()
def pivot(df, region=None):
    d = df if region is None else df[df["Region"] == region]
    return d.groupby("Region")["Amount"].sum().to_dict()

print(pivot(cache, "North"))
sales.loc[len(sales)] = ["North", 25]
print(pivot(cache, "North"))
cache = sales.copy()
print(pivot(cache, "North"))
print(pivot(cache))
```

```output
{'North': 70}
{'North': 70}
{'North': 95}
{'North': 95, 'South': 10}
```

The variable `cache` plays the part of the pivot cache. After the new row is added, the pivot still shows 70 for North. After the refresh (`cache = sales.copy()`) it shows 95. The slicer is the filter argument, and clearing it shows all regions.

## Real-world applications

- **Fast ad-hoc answers.** A manager asks for revenue by region and product; a pivot gives the grid in under a minute and the same fields can be dragged to answer the next question (Example 1).
- **Monthly and quarterly reporting.** Grouping a date field into months and years turns a transaction log into a trend table ready for a chart (Example 3).
- **Reconciliation.** Comparing a pivot grand total with `SUM` of the source and with `SUMIFS` totals shows whether any row was dropped, for instance because of a text number (Example 2).
- **Unit economics.** A calculated field gives a weighted average price or a margin per region, avoiding the bias of averaging row ratios (Example 4).

## Daily review

### Key takeaways

- A PivotTable summarises a source Table with Rows, Columns, Values and Filters; Excel works from a pivot cache.
- Values default to Count when the field holds text or blanks; fix the data, not the label.
- Group dates by Months and Years together, and numbers by start, end and step.
- A calculated field works on summed fields, which gives a ratio of totals.
- A pivot is only as current as the last refresh; a Table source picks up new rows on refresh.

### Common mistakes

- Reading "Count of Amount" as a total because a text value or a blank sits in the field.
- Grouping dates by Months only, so the same month of different years is merged.
- Using a plain range as the source and not seeing new rows after a refresh.
- Expecting a calculated field to average row ratios.
- Typing over a pivot cell or building formulas on the visible cell address that move when the pivot changes shape; use `GETPIVOTDATA`.
- Forgetting to refresh after correcting the source data.
